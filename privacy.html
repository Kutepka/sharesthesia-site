Privacy policy

Last updated: 18 November 2026

The short version

Sharesthesia builds your profile from what you really listen to and really play, and lets people with the same taste find you. To do that it needs to know what plays on your phone. That is the only reason it exists, so this page is mostly about what that means in practice.

We don't sell your data. There are no ads and no ad trackers in the app. We don't read your messages, record sound, read your contacts or track your location. You can see everything the app keeps about you, switch each source off, and delete your account in two taps.

Who is behind the app

Sharesthesia is made and run by Serghei, an independent developer in Moldova. There is no company behind it yet. If anything here is unclear, or you want to ask about your data, write to sharesthesia@gmail.com and you will get a reply from the person who wrote the app.

What the app collects, and why

Your account

You sign in with Google. From Google we receive your email address, your Google name and a Google account id. The email is how the app recognises you; it is shown only to you, in Settings. The Google name is stored with the sign-in record and never shown to anyone.

In the app you choose a username and add up to three photos. Optionally: a country, a city, a short bio, a favourite song and a favourite game, and links to where you can be found (Telegram, Discord, Instagram and the like). Your phone's time zone is saved automatically so that "today" means your today.

What you listen to Spins

The app has a Listener. It uses Android's notification access, because that is the way Android lets an app see what a music player is playing. It does not read your notifications: it looks only at the media card that a player shows while something plays.

For each play from a music player it keeps: the title, artist, album, the track's length, how many seconds actually played, when it started and ended, and which app played it. For anything else that plays (a video in a browser, a voice message in a messenger) it keeps only which app, when, and for how long, never a title or any words. Nobody else ever sees those entries, and they never count toward anything.

If you connect Last.fm, we store the Last.fm username you typed, your top artists, tracks and albums with your play counts, and your scrobble count per day.

The Listener is optional, off until you turn it on, and you can turn it off at any time in Android's notification access settings. Nothing is counted while you are signed out.

What you play Runs

If you connect Steam, you sign in on Steam's own site and Steam tells us your public Steam id. We never see your Steam password. From your public Steam profile we read your game library and playtimes, what you played recently, and the game you are in right now. Your library needs to be public on Steam for this to work.

If you turn on mobile games, the app uses Android's usage access to count how long you play the games you ticked. The games Android marks as games are ticked for you, and you can untick any of them in Settings. We keep the name of each ticked game and your minutes in it per day. Nothing about an app you haven't ticked leaves your phone.

What happens inside the app

We keep a record of what you do in the app so that the app can work: the signals you send on the Sonar, pings sent and received, who you chose to see, Sparks earned and spent, quests claimed, invites, blocks and reports, and which notices you have read. From all of the above the app works out your week, your Spins, Runs and Sparks, your skills and levels, and a short summary of your taste.

Technical things

Our server keeps a short log line per request (your user id, which function, how long it took, whether it worked). Your sign-in sessions keep your IP address and your device's browser string. If you allow notifications, we keep a push token so the app can reach your phone. The app's update system sends an anonymous install id when it checks for updates. There is no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising SDK in the app.

What other people see

Showing your taste to people with the same taste is the point of the app, so part of your profile is visible to other signed-in people. There is no public web profile; nobody who is signed out can see anything.

Always visible on your profile: your username, photos, country and city if you added them, your bio, your favourites, the artists and tracks the Listener heard most, your games, your skills and their levels, how many people pinged you, and your taste summary.

Visible by default, and you can switch it off in Settings → Visibility: your week (the top artists, how many artists and tracks, the last tracks, genres).

Only if you switch it on: what you are listening to right now, and the Steam game you are in right now. Both are shown only while they are fresh, for about twenty minutes.

Only to mutuals: your "Find me on" links. They open only after you and another person have pinged each other.

Only to you: how much time you listened and played. Other people see your artists, covers and levels, not your minutes and hours.

When the Sonar finds someone for you, you both see the one real thing you share, for example that you both have the same artist on repeat this week. That is the only thing a stranger learns about you before you choose to ping.

Nobody else ever sees: your email, your Steam id, your Last.fm username, your raw plays, your balances, your quests, whom you revealed or passed, your blocks, or anything the Listener heard from apps that are not music players.

Blocking someone takes you both out of each other's Sonar, pings, mutuals and notices, and hides from that person what you two share, your "now" lines, your links and your skills.

Photos and the automatic check

Every photo you upload is checked automatically before anyone else can see it, to keep sexual, violent and similar images off profiles. The check is done by OpenAI's moderation service: the photo is fetched once through a link that works for one minute. No name, username, email or account id goes with it. A photo stays private until it passes. If it doesn't, you simply see that it was declined.

Sharesthesia is for adults. By using it you confirm you are 18 or older. Anyone can report a profile that looks like it belongs to a minor, and such profiles are removed.

Who works for us

These services store or move your data on our behalf. They act on our instructions and don't use your data for their own purposes.

  • Supabase: the database, sign-in, photo storage and the server functions. Data is stored in the EU (Frankfurt).
  • Expo: delivers app updates and push notifications.
  • Google: Google sign-in, and Firebase Cloud Messaging to deliver notifications to Android. Google Play handles installs and updates.
  • OpenAI: the automatic photo check described above.

These services we ask for information. They are independent and have their own policies:

  • Steam (Valve) gets your Steam id, so we can read your public library.
  • Last.fm gets your Last.fm username, so we can read your top lists and counts. Catalogue lookups (artist and track names) carry nothing about you. Music data powered by AudioScrobbler.
  • Deezer and RAWG answer catalogue lookups by name. They get the names and search words only, from our server, never your id.
  • Covers, artwork and 30-second previews are loaded by your phone straight from Deezer, Last.fm, Steam and RAWG, so those servers see your phone's IP address, like any web page would.

What we don't do

We don't sell or rent your data. We don't show ads or let advertisers track you. We don't read notifications, messages, playlists or libraries on your phone. We don't record audio. We don't read your contacts: invites go through Android's share sheet. We don't ask for your location: the only location-ish things we hold are your time zone and whatever country and city you type yourself.

How long we keep it

Everything is kept while your account exists. Server logs and sign-in sessions are kept for a limited time and then cleared on their own. Catalogue lookups are cached under the names and words searched, not under you.

Your choices

  • Turn the Listener off in Android's notification access settings. New plays stop at once.
  • Switch individual players on or off in Settings → Sources, so only the ones you want count.
  • Disconnect Steam or Last.fm in Settings. The items they brought leave your profile and we forget the connected account.
  • Untick any mobile game, or turn mobile games off entirely.
  • Hide your week, and keep "listening now" and "playing now" off.
  • Remove any item or photo from your profile.
  • Block anyone. Report anyone.
  • Delete your account, in the app or on the account deletion page.
  • Write to us about anything above, including to ask what we hold about you.

Deleting your account

Settings → Delete account removes your profile and everything attached to it right away: photos, items, plays, your week, your skills and levels, Spins, Runs and Sparks, pings, mutuals, notices, quests, invites, blocks, push tokens, your Steam library copy, and the sign-in account itself. Two things remain for a while: reports you made stay without your name on them, and server logs keep your old user id until they expire. The same happens if you ask by email on the deletion page.

Changes to this page

When something here changes, the date at the top changes with it. If a change matters, for example a new source of data or a new service working for us, you will see a notice in the app before it takes effect.
